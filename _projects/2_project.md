This project aims to uncover the fundamental mechanisms behind unconventional superconductivity in non-honeycomb moiré lattices, a rapidly emerging frontier in condensed matter physics with direct relevance for quantum materials design. While significant advances have been made in understanding superconductivity in honeycomb graphene moiré systems, unconventional pairing in alternative lattices such as kagome, square, and triangular moiré superlattices remains poorly understood. These systems--exemplified by kagome metals AV₃Sb₅ (A = K, Rb, Cs), Nb₃X₈ (X = Cl, Br, I); FeGe), square-lattice oxides like NdNiO₂ and Ge/SnX (X = S, Se), and triangular compounds such as Mn₃Ge--feature uniquely intertwined geometric frustration, multi-orbital interactions, and flat-band electronic topology that drive strongly correlated phases and topological superconductivity. Recent experiments on CsV₃Sb₅ reveal fully gapped superconductivity with rotational symmetry breaking and anisotropic multi-gap structures, highlighting complex pairing beyond conventional BCS mechanisms. Twisted trilayer WSe₂ at small twist angles shows superconductivity emerging near half-filling in flat Chern bands, achieving critical temperatures approaching 220 mK, while heterostructures such as NbSe₂/CrBr₃ evidence moiré phonons that soften electron-phonon coupling and yield unconventional superconducting states. Such findings emphasize the role of moiré-enabled topology and frustration in shaping superconductivity, implicating exotic pairing symmetries including chiral and topological orders. The project employs a hierarchical computational approach over three years: first, den- sity functional theory (DFT) calculations incorporating spin-orbit coupling and Hubbard U corrections will characterize electronic and phonon structures of target moiré systems, simulating large supercells to capture realistic twist angles and flat-band features. Subsequently, Wannier-based tight-binding models will reconstruct low-energy Hamiltonians enabling large-scale Brillouin zone sampling to quantify Fermi surface nesting, topological invariants (e.g., Chern numbers), and flat band phenomena. Advanced many-body methods--including random phase approximation (RPA) and quantum Monte Carlo--will analyze spin and charge susceptibilities, solving Eliashberg gap equations to predict superconducting order parameter symmetries and transition temperatures under varied doping, strain, and pressure. Ultimately, this research will illuminate the conditions under which frustration, multi-orbital interactions, and topology converge to yield new superconducting states, providing predictive guidelines for engineering moiré quantum materials with tailored electronic and topological functionalities. The insights gained will contribute to fundamental condensed matter physics and inform future quantum device technologies.
